A contractor calls your branch and says, "I need a two-inch brass ball valve, full port, threaded, for a commercial water line." With a clear request like that, your experienced counterperson can probably find the right SKU in a few minutes using the ERP system or a catalog.

Now consider a different call: "Hey, I need one of those valves, the kind with the yellow handle, you sold me a bunch of them last year for the school job." Same product category. Completely different level of difficulty. The second request is the one that actually represents how most counter interactions go.

This article compares two approaches to handling both kinds of requests: the traditional catalog lookup process that distribution has relied on for decades, and the AI-powered product identification that is beginning to replace it.

## How Traditional Catalog Lookup Works

<img src="/media/posts/ai-product-identification-vs-catalog-lookup/01-hero.svg" alt="Split illustration comparing traditional catalog lookup on the left showing physical catalogs, ERP screens, and phone calls with AI product identification on the right showing a single search interface returning instant results" className="w-full rounded-2xl border border-black/10 dark:border-white/10 shadow-sm" loading="lazy" />

Traditional product lookup in distribution follows a well-worn path that most counterpeople know by heart.

**Step 1: Interpret the request.** The employee listens to the customer and mentally translates their description into searchable terms. This requires product knowledge, familiarity with trade language, and sometimes a bit of detective work.

**Step 2: Search the ERP.** The employee types keywords into the ERP system's search function. Most ERP search tools in distribution use basic keyword matching, which means the search term needs to closely match how the product was entered into the system. Search for "ball valve" and you might find it. Search for "shut-off valve" and you might get nothing, even though they are the same product.

**Step 3: Browse results.** ERP keyword searches often return hundreds of results. The employee scrolls through, filtering mentally by size, material, brand, and connection type. This is where product knowledge matters most, and where newer employees struggle the most.

**Step 4: Consult secondary sources.** If the ERP search fails, the employee turns to manufacturer catalogs, cross-reference guides, colleagues, or phone calls to manufacturer reps. Each additional source adds minutes to the process.

**Step 5: Verify and quote.** Once a potential match is found, the employee confirms availability and pricing in the ERP, then relays the information to the customer.

This process works. It has worked for decades. But it is slow, it depends heavily on individual knowledge, and it breaks down when the request is vague, when the employee is new, or when the counter is busy.

## How AI Product Identification Works

AI product identification compresses the entire traditional workflow into a single step. The employee types or speaks the customer's request, in whatever language the customer used, and the system returns the matching products from the distributor's own catalog.

The key difference is in how the search works. Traditional ERP search matches keywords against product descriptions. AI search understands intent and meaning. It knows that "two-inch brass ball valve, full port, threaded" and "2" FPT ball valve, brass, full port" are the same request even though the words are different.

More importantly, it handles the vague requests that traditional search cannot parse at all. "The valve with the yellow handle from last year" becomes a searchable query when the AI understands that yellow handles typically indicate gas-rated ball valves and can narrow results accordingly.

## Side-by-Side: Five Real Scenarios

Let's compare how each approach handles the kinds of requests that actually come across the counter every day.

### Scenario 1: Clear Request with Part Number

**Customer says:** "I need a Watts LFN45BM1 pressure reducing valve."

**Traditional lookup:** Employee types the part number into the ERP. If it is in the system and the entry matches exactly, results appear in 30 seconds to 1 minute. If the part number has a dash, space, or revision letter that does not match the ERP entry, the search may fail and require a manual catalog check.

**AI identification:** Employee types or pastes the part number. The system identifies the product instantly, shows availability and pricing, and also surfaces compatible accessories like pressure gauges or expansion tanks. Time: under 15 seconds.

### Scenario 2: Description Without a Part Number

**Customer says:** "I need a three-quarter-inch copper press coupling, no stop."

**Traditional lookup:** Employee searches for "press coupling" in the ERP. Results return 200+ items across all sizes and types. Employee filters mentally or scrolls to find 3/4-inch, no-stop, copper variants. Time: 3 to 7 minutes depending on system and employee experience.

**AI identification:** Employee types the request as stated. System returns the matching SKUs immediately, filtered to 3/4-inch press couplings without stops in the distributor's stocked brands. Time: under 20 seconds.

### Scenario 3: Trade Slang and Shorthand

**Customer says:** "Give me a box of shark bites, three-quarter, and some dielectric unions for the water heater."

**Traditional lookup:** "Shark bite" is a brand name (SharkBite by RWC) used generically for push-fit connectors. The employee needs to know this, then search for the specific push-fit fittings the branch stocks, which may be SharkBite or a competing brand. "Dielectric unions" requires knowing the water heater connection sizes. Time: 5 to 10 minutes.

**AI identification:** System recognizes "shark bites" as push-fit connectors, identifies 3/4-inch variants in stock, and pulls dielectric unions in standard water heater sizes. Time: under 30 seconds.

### Scenario 4: Vague Request Requiring Interpretation

**Customer says:** "I need that thing that goes between the water meter and the house, the one the city inspector makes you put in."

**Traditional lookup:** The employee needs to recognize this as a request for a backflow preventer or dual check valve, typically required by code at the water service entrance. Newer employees may not make this connection. Even experienced ones need to determine the correct size and type based on local code requirements. Time: 5 to 15 minutes, assuming the employee knows what the customer is describing.

**AI identification:** System interprets the description, recognizes the application context (water service entrance, code compliance), and returns appropriate backflow preventers and dual check valves in common residential sizes. Time: under 30 seconds.

### Scenario 5: Cross-Reference Request

**Customer says:** "I need the equivalent of a Grundfos UP15-42F circulator, but in whatever brand you stock."

**Traditional lookup:** Employee searches for the Grundfos model, identifies the specifications (flow rate, head pressure, flange size), then searches for equivalents from stocked brands like Taco or Bell & Gossett. May require checking cross-reference guides or calling the manufacturer rep. Time: 10 to 30 minutes.

**AI identification:** System identifies the Grundfos pump specs and immediately cross-references to equivalent models from brands in the distributor's catalog, showing side-by-side spec comparisons. Time: under 30 seconds.

If even a quarter of those interactions involve the kind of complex lookups where AI delivers significant time savings, the cumulative effect on branch productivity is substantial.

## Beyond Speed: Accuracy and Consistency

Time savings are the most visible benefit, but accuracy improvements may be more valuable in the long run.

Traditional catalog lookup accuracy depends almost entirely on the individual employee. An experienced counterperson with 20 years in plumbing supply will find the right part faster and more reliably than a new hire with six months of experience. This creates an inconsistent customer experience that varies by shift, by branch, and by who happens to answer the phone.

AI product identification delivers consistent accuracy regardless of who is using it. The new hire and the veteran get the same search results from the same query. This does not mean the veteran's knowledge becomes irrelevant. It means the floor of performance across the team rises dramatically.

This consistency matters especially for [evaluating AI tools for your distribution business](/blog/evaluate-ai-tools-distribution), where accuracy and reliability across varying skill levels should be key criteria.

## Employee Satisfaction Impact

There is an underappreciated dimension to this comparison. Employee satisfaction.

Counterpeople who spend significant portions of their day on frustrating lookups, scrolling through ERP screens, flipping through catalogs, waiting for callbacks from manufacturer reps, report lower job satisfaction than those who can resolve customer requests quickly and confidently.

For newer employees, the frustration is even more acute. They know they are slow. They know the customer is waiting. They know their more experienced colleagues could have answered the question in half the time. This gap in capability is one of the factors driving turnover in distribution counter roles.

AI product identification does not eliminate the need for product knowledge. But it removes the most tedious parts of the job and lets employees focus on the work that actually requires human judgment: understanding the customer's project, recommending the right solution, and building the relationship that keeps them coming back.

## Not About Eliminating Catalogs

It is worth being direct about what AI product identification is not. It is not about throwing away your catalogs, dismantling your ERP, or pretending that product knowledge does not matter.

Catalogs are still valuable for detailed specification review, installation guidance, and engineering reference. ERP systems remain the backbone of inventory management, pricing, and order processing. And product knowledge is still what separates a great counterperson from an adequate one.

What AI changes is accessibility. The information inside your catalogs and ERP becomes accessible to every employee, regardless of their experience level, without requiring them to know exactly where to look or what terminology to use. The catalog is not going away. It is becoming searchable in a way it never was before.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a traditional catalog lookup take compared to AI?
A traditional catalog lookup for a straightforward request with a known part number takes 2 to 5 minutes. For vague or complex requests that require browsing catalogs, calling manufacturers, or consulting colleagues, it can take 10 to 30 minutes or more. AI product identification typically resolves the same requests in under 30 seconds, including cross-references and alternative suggestions.

### Does AI product identification replace the need for catalogs entirely?
No. Catalogs remain valuable reference materials, especially for detailed engineering specifications, installation instructions, and product selection guides. AI does not eliminate catalogs. It makes the information inside them accessible without requiring employees to know exactly where to look or what terminology to use.

### What happens when AI cannot find a match?
A well-designed AI system will tell you it cannot find a confident match rather than guessing. It may suggest related products or ask clarifying questions. This is actually an advantage over traditional methods, where an uncertain employee might provide an incorrect match without realizing the error.

### Can AI handle requests described in trade slang or shorthand?
Yes. Purpose-built AI for distribution is trained to understand trade terminology, regional slang, and industry shorthand. It knows that a plumber asking for a "fernco" is looking for a flexible coupling, that "unistrut" is a brand name used generically for metal framing channels, and that "shark bites" refers to push-fit connectors regardless of manufacturer.

### How does AI product identification handle discontinued parts?
AI systems connected to your catalog data can identify when a requested part has been discontinued and automatically suggest current replacements or equivalents. This is faster than discovering a part is discontinued only after searching for it, then starting a new search for alternatives.

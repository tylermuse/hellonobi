If you run distribution operations and you have been researching AI-powered search, you have probably come across HawkSearch. It is one of the most recognized names in B2B eCommerce search, and for good reason. HawkSearch is excellent at what it does.

But what it does might not be what you need most.

This article is not a takedown of HawkSearch. It is a clarification of use cases. HawkSearch and tools like it solve the customer-facing search problem. Many distributors have an equally pressing, and often larger, problem on the employee side. Understanding the difference between these two problems is essential before you invest in any search or AI technology.

## What HawkSearch Does Well

<img src="/media/posts/hawksearch-alternatives-distributors/01-hero.svg" alt="Split illustration showing customer-facing eCommerce search on the left with a website search bar, filters, and product grid versus employee-facing AI on the right with a counter interaction, natural language input, and instant part identification" className="w-full rounded-2xl border border-black/10 dark:border-white/10 shadow-sm" loading="lazy" />

[HawkSearch](https://www.hawksearch.com/) is a B2B eCommerce search and recommendation platform. It has been recognized by [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/3991608) as a notable vendor in the digital commerce search space, and it is used by distributors across multiple verticals.

Its core capabilities include:

**Faceted navigation.** HawkSearch lets online shoppers narrow product results using filters like brand, size, material, price, and custom attributes. For a distributor with 100,000 SKUs, this kind of structured browsing is essential for the eCommerce experience.

**Autocomplete and suggestions.** As customers type in the search bar, HawkSearch suggests products, categories, and popular searches. This reduces the friction between what the customer types and what they find.

**Merchandising rules.** HawkSearch allows merchandisers to boost, bury, or pin specific products in search results. This gives distributors control over what appears first when customers search for competitive categories.

**Analytics and reporting.** The platform tracks what customers search for, what they click on, and where they drop off. This data helps distributors optimize their product catalog and website experience.

These capabilities are genuinely valuable for distributors with eCommerce websites. If your customers are buying online through your site, a tool like HawkSearch improves their ability to find and purchase products without calling your branch.

## The Problem HawkSearch Doesn't Solve

Here is the scenario that HawkSearch is not designed for:

A contractor calls your branch and says, "Hey, I need some of those push fittings, the ones that work on both copper and PEX. Three-quarter inch. And I also need whatever goes with it to stub out a water heater."

This request is not a keyword search. There are no filters to apply. The customer did not type anything into a search bar. An employee heard a vague verbal description and needs to interpret it, identify the products, check availability, and give the contractor a total.

This is the employee-facing use case, and it is fundamentally different from eCommerce site search. It requires:

- **Natural language interpretation.** The request is conversational, not structured. No keywords, no filters, no facets.
- **Trade language understanding.** "Push fittings" could mean SharkBite, ProPress, or any number of push-fit connector brands. "Stub out a water heater" implies specific fittings, connectors, and possibly flex lines that depend on local code requirements.
- **Cross-referencing.** The contractor might be asking for a brand you do not carry, and the employee needs to find the equivalent from a brand you stock.
- **Vague request resolution.** The customer does not know the exact product. They are describing what it does, not what it is called. The employee needs to bridge that gap.

This is not what eCommerce search tools are built for. It is not a criticism of HawkSearch. It is a different problem entirely.

## Customer-Facing vs. Employee-Facing: Two Different Jobs

The distinction between customer-facing and employee-facing search is not about quality. It is about the nature of the interaction.

**Customer-facing search** is designed for self-service. The customer sits at a computer, types queries, applies filters, browses categories, and makes selections. The interface is visual, structured, and asynchronous. The customer can take their time. They can refine their search. They can compare products side by side.

**Employee-facing AI** is designed for real-time interpretation. The employee is on the phone or at the counter. The request comes in natural language, often incomplete, sometimes using incorrect terminology. There is a customer waiting. The employee needs to identify the right product in seconds, not minutes.

Here is how the use cases compare:

| Dimension | Customer-Facing (HawkSearch, etc.) | Employee-Facing (AI Copilot) |
|---|---|---|
| User | Website visitor | Counterperson, inside sales |
| Input method | Typed keywords with filters | Verbal description, often vague |
| Query structure | Structured, can be refined | Unstructured, one-shot |
| Time pressure | Low (customer browses at their pace) | High (customer is waiting) |
| Product knowledge | Customer has some idea what they want | Customer may not know the product name |
| Cross-referencing | Rarely needed (customer searches by spec) | Frequently needed (customer asks for wrong brand) |
| Trade language | Limited (customers use common terms) | Heavy (contractors use trade slang) |

According to the [National Association of Wholesaler-Distributors](https://www.naw.org/), 60% to 80% of B2B distribution revenue still flows through employee-assisted channels: counter sales, phone orders, and inside sales interactions. Even distributors with mature eCommerce platforms find that their most complex and highest-value transactions happen through human employees.

This means that for most distributors, the employee-facing problem is actually the larger opportunity. Improving employee productivity and accuracy at the counter affects more revenue than optimizing the eCommerce search bar.

## The eCommerce Search Landscape

HawkSearch is not the only option for customer-facing search. If you are evaluating eCommerce search tools, here are the primary alternatives:

**[Algolia](https://www.algolia.com/).** Algolia is a developer-friendly search platform known for its speed and relevance. It powers search for companies across eCommerce, media, and SaaS. For distributors, Algolia offers strong autocomplete, typo tolerance, and customizable ranking rules. It is particularly popular with distributors who have in-house development teams that want granular control over the search experience.

**[Coveo](https://www.coveo.com/).** Coveo positions itself as an AI-powered relevance platform. It goes beyond basic search to include content recommendations, personalization, and analytics. Coveo is more commonly found in enterprise deployments where the distributor has a large catalog and wants machine learning-driven relevance tuning. It is a heavier implementation than Algolia but offers more sophisticated AI capabilities.

**[Searchspring](https://searchspring.com/).** Searchspring focuses on merchandising and conversion optimization. It combines search, product recommendations, and visual merchandising tools. For distributors whose eCommerce strategy includes active merchandising and promotional campaigns, Searchspring offers a strong feature set.

All of these tools share the same fundamental design: they are built for customers interacting with a website. They accept typed input, apply structured filters, and return visual product results. They are excellent at this job. None of them is designed for the employee who just heard a contractor say, "I need that whatchamacallit for the main line."

## Where Both Tools Fit

The most effective technology stack for a modern distributor includes both layers.

**Layer 1: Customer-facing search** for your eCommerce website. This is where HawkSearch, Algolia, Coveo, or Searchspring add value. They help the 20% to 40% of your business that flows through digital self-service channels.

**Layer 2: Employee-facing AI** for your counter, phone, and inside sales teams. This is where purpose-built AI copilots add value. They help the 60% to 80% of your business that still requires human employees to interpret customer needs and find the right products.

These tools are complementary, not competitive. You would not use HawkSearch to help your counterperson interpret a vague phone call. You would not use an employee-facing AI copilot to power your website's search bar. They solve different problems for different users in different contexts.

The mistake some distributors make is investing heavily in eCommerce search and assuming it will also solve their employee productivity challenges. It will not. The interfaces are different. The input types are different. The required AI capabilities are different.

## What to Look for in Employee-Facing AI

If you have already invested in customer-facing search and are now looking at the employee side, here are the capabilities that matter most:

**Catalog-grounded results.** Every result must come from your product catalog. No hallucinated part numbers. No suggestions for products you do not stock. This is the foundational requirement that separates purpose-built tools from general AI, as we discussed in our article on [why ChatGPT fails for part identification](/blog/chatgpt-for-part-identification).

**Natural language understanding.** The tool must handle conversational, unstructured input. Employees should be able to type or speak the customer's request exactly as it was said, without reformulating it into search-engine-friendly keywords.

**Trade language fluency.** The tool must understand that "fernco" means flexible coupling, that "LB" is a conduit body, and that "mini-split" could refer to dozens of different configurations. This requires training specific to distribution trade terminology, not just general language understanding.

**Cross-referencing.** When a customer asks for Brand A and you carry Brand B, the tool should identify the equivalent automatically, based on specifications, not just pre-built lookup tables. For more on this capability, see our article on [cross-referencing parts across manufacturers](/blog/cross-reference-parts-manufacturers).

**Speed.** Counter interactions are time-sensitive. The tool needs to return results in seconds, not minutes. If it takes longer than a manual lookup, employees will not use it.

**Minimal training requirement.** Counterpeople and inside sales staff should be able to start using the tool within minutes. If it requires hours of training or a fundamentally different workflow, adoption will be a challenge.

## The Bottom Line

HawkSearch is a strong product for the problem it was built to solve. If you need better eCommerce search on your website, it deserves a place on your shortlist alongside Algolia, Coveo, and Searchspring.

But if your primary challenge is employee productivity, if your counterpeople spend too long on complex lookups, if your new hires take too long to ramp, if your best product experts are retiring and taking their knowledge with them, then you need a different category of tool entirely.

The employee-facing AI copilot is not a replacement for eCommerce search. It is the other half of the equation. And for most distributors, it is the half that affects the majority of their revenue.
